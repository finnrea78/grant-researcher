## Research Strands

### Strand 1: Yoruba Masquerade and Performance

This is Rea's dominant research thread. It spans 30 years of fieldwork in Ikole Ekiti. He has built the most detailed account in the literature of the Epa and Egungun masquerade traditions in this single community. His approach moves from structural and formal analysis toward performance theory and political interpretation.

**Key works:**
- "The Epa-type masquerades of Ekiti: A structural approach" (2019), *African Arts* — definitive structural account of Epa typology
- "Making history: The modernity of masquerade in Ikole Ekiti" (2008), *African Arts* — argues masquerade is a site of historical self-making
- "On Staging Performance: The masquerade festival of Ikole Ekiti" (2007), *Journal of African Cultural Studies* — performance theory applied to Egungun
- "No condition is permanent: the Egigun masquerades of Ikole Ekiti" (2007), *The Nigerian Field* — ethnographic baseline study
- "Rationalising Culture: youth, elites and masquerade politics" (1998), *Africa* — early career statement on masquerade as political arena
- "Masks and Styles: Yoruba masquerade in a comparative perspective" (2001), Horniman Museum — comparative framework
- **In peer review:** "No Event, No History: Masquerade Performance and Politics in Nigeria" (James Currey) — monograph synthesising this strand

### Strand 2: Nigerian Modernism and Contemporary African Art

Rea's second major strand examines how Yoruba/Nigerian traditions articulate with modernism — both as practitioners negotiating modernity and as art-historical category. Recent work in *Art Bulletin* and *NKa* marks a significant development in this direction.

**Key works:**
- "The King's Horseman: Portraiture and politics in Yoruba Nigeria" (2022), *The Art Bulletin* — high-impact intervention placing Yoruba portraiture in modernist debate
- "Street To Mbari: Jacob Lawrence and the influence of the 'International' on a local modernism" (2022), *NKa Journal of Contemporary African Art* — traces transnational artistic exchange
- "When was Yoruba art in Nigerian Modernism" (2025), Tate Gallery Publications — positions Yoruba art within Nigerian modernism debates
- "Bamigboye, master of Epa: A historical context" (2022), Yale University Press — monographic-scale chapter on a canonical sculptor
- "Finding your contemporary: Art and aesthetics in Nigeria now" (2008), Tate Liverpool/Liverpool UP — early statement on contemporary Nigerian aesthetics

### Strand 3: Anthropology of Art, Materiality, and Theory

A methodological and theoretical strand running beneath Rea's empirical work. He contributes to interdisciplinary debates on materiality, the relationship between sculpture and archaeology, and the positioning of anthropology within postcolonial studies.

**Key works:**
- "Materialising materiality – the space in-between" (2017), Bloomsbury — theoretical essay on the ontology of material objects
- "Anthropology and Post-colonialism" (2010), Wiley-Blackwell — overview contribution to postcolonial literary studies reader
- "Shared sites and misleading affinities: sculpture as archaeology and archaeology as sculpture" (2011), Ashgate — methodological critique of disciplinary boundary-drawing
- "Anthropology" (2004), OUP — annual critical overview contribution
- "The Sculptors of Ekiti: The context of creativity" (submitted), in Picton Ed., *William Fagg and African Art* — connects creativity theory to Ekiti practice

---

### Strand 4: Heritage, Display, and African Cultural Institutions

An applied and public-facing strand — increasingly prominent in Rea's current work — engaging with how African art is displayed, collected, and institutionalised. This strand is most visible in consultancy roles and the John Randle Museum project.

**Key works:**
- **In preparation:** "Africa displaying / Displaying Africa" (Cambridge UP Elements series) — commissioned synthesis of this strand
- Vatican Museum consultant, Africa Displays (2020)
- British Library Advisory Committee, "West Africa: word, symbol, song" (2014–2016)
- "From Ekiti to Ebute Metta" (2020), Gelda Verlag — African art and Christian modernity as display problem
- Exhibitions curated: *Yoruba textiles* (Leeds, 2012–13), *Truth and Beauty* (Harewood House, 2007)
