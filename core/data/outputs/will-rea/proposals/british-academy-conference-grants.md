## 1. Project Framing

The British Academy Conference Grant funds international academic conferences, workshops, and symposia in the humanities and social sciences held in the UK. Rea holds a prior BA Conference Grant (2008, £400). The scheme is particularly well-suited to Rea's situation now: his research programme intersects several active scholarly conversations (African art history, decolonisation, museum studies, masquerade performance) that would benefit from a UK-based convening event.

This is a lower-competition route than the fellowship schemes. The BA Conference Grant is a tactical opportunity to build international scholarly networks, create visibility for Rea's programme, and generate a publication output — all of which also strengthen future fellowship applications.

## 2. Conference/Workshop Concept Suggestions

### Option A: Workshop on Yoruba Masquerade — Scholarship, Methods, and New Directions

A 2-day workshop at the University of Leeds bringing together leading scholars in Yoruba masquerade studies from the UK, Nigeria, Europe, and North America. Papers would address the current state of scholarship, methodological debates, and emerging directions — particularly the intersection of performance studies, political anthropology, and art history. Outputs: edited proceedings or a special issue of *African Arts* or *Journal of African Cultural Studies*. This workshop would directly support Rea's two manuscripts and establish Leeds as a convening centre for this field. Invite 10–15 international specialists; 1 day for scholarly exchange and 1 day for public programme at the Henry Moore Institute or Leeds Art Gallery.

### Option B: Symposium on African Heritage in European Collections

A symposium examining how African art objects are displayed, interpreted, and contested in UK and European museum contexts — the scholarly conversation around which *Africa displaying / Displaying Africa* (Cambridge UP) is positioned. Could be co-organised with the Henry Moore Institute (HMI) or the Horniman Museum. Outputs: a published symposium report or edited volume. This event connects Rea's academic programme to the live public debate about decolonisation of museum collections — strong BA relevance.

### Option C: International Workshop on African Modernism and the Global 1960s

A workshop addressing Nigerian and wider African modernism in global context, drawing on Rea's *Art Bulletin* 2022 paper and the NKa 2022 essay. Participants would include art historians, postcolonial scholars, and museum professionals from Africa, Europe, and North America. Links to the Tate Modern and Tate Liverpool programmes (Rea contributed to both). Output: edited proceedings or a proposed journal special issue.

**Recommendation:** Option A (Yoruba Masquerade workshop) or Option B (African Heritage in European Collections). Both connect directly to the two manuscripts in progress and produce a tangible output that advances the research programme.

## 4. Methodology Notes

Conference grants are assessed on: the intellectual case for the event, the quality and scope of invited participants, the practical delivery plan (venue, budget, programme), and the outputs the event will generate. The application should name confirmed or prospective international speakers — not just disciplinary coverage but specific scholars whose participation makes the event necessary and valuable.

For Option A or B, co-organisation with HMI or Horniman strengthens the case: it demonstrates institutional partnership and provides a ready-made public audience for an attached public event.

## 6. Weaknesses to Address

- **Potential concern:** The prior BA Conference Grant was only £400 — this was a very small award. → **How to address:** Acknowledge the prior award and use it as a signal of established BA relationship. The scale difference (£400 to up to £15,000) reflects a different type of event, not a different category of application.
- **Potential concern:** The scheme funds events "in the UK" — international co-organisation needs to be handled carefully. → **How to address:** The event itself must be UK-based. International participants can be funded to travel to the UK event from the BA grant. Do not propose events taking place outside the UK.
- **Potential concern:** Budget for an international workshop exceeds the grant maximum. → **How to address:** State explicitly what co-funding is in place (institutional contributions from Leeds or partner institutions). Underwriting the event from a single source at this scale is unusual; presenting a co-funded model is more credible.
