## 2. Research Question Suggestions

### Option A: Completing the Ekiti Masquerade Monograph (recommended)

Frame the fellowship around a single, defined deliverable: completing the "No Event, No History: Masquerade Performance and Politics in Nigeria" monograph for James Currey Publications. The manuscript is already in peer review, meaning the fellowship would fund the revision and completion phase — one of the most compelling narratives available. The argument is that 25 years of interrupted teaching prevented sustained completion of a manuscript that represents the most comprehensive account of Yoruba masquerade in the Anglophone scholarly record. The 2–3 year fellowship would allow revision in response to peer review, final fieldwork verification where needed, and delivery of the completed manuscript.

### Option B: Dual completion — masquerade monograph and Cambridge UP Elements

Frame the fellowship around completing BOTH manuscripts simultaneously. The James Currey masquerade monograph is in peer review; the Cambridge UP *Africa displaying / Displaying Africa* was commissioned and is at advanced draft stage. Both are at the point where sustained writing time — rather than more research — is the bottleneck. A 3-year fellowship would allow Year 1 for the James Currey revision, Year 2 for the Cambridge UP completion, and Year 3 for a new research programme (comparative display studies, or the Bamigboye/Ekiti comparative follow-on). This framing demonstrates a full and ambitious research programme, not just a completion project.

### Option C: Nigerian Modernism — A New Research Programme

Frame the fellowship around a forward-looking project: a major study of Nigerian modernism and the international circulation of Yoruba visual culture, building on Rea's 2022 *Art Bulletin* paper and the NKa essay. This positions the fellowship not as monograph completion but as establishing a new research programme. The manuscripts already in progress become preliminary work rather than the primary deliverable. Stronger for demonstrating future ambition, but weaker on the "clear deliverable" criterion that Leverhulme reviewers typically value.

**Recommendation:** Option B (dual completion) is the strongest — it foregrounds the two near-complete manuscripts as compelling proof that the investment will yield concrete outputs, while showing sufficient scope for a 3-year award.

## 4. Methodology Notes

The Leverhulme application does not require methodological innovation — it requires a compelling description of a research programme that is already methodologically established. Rea's combination of long-term ethnographic fieldwork, formal art-historical analysis, and theoretical engagement (postcolonial theory, anthropology of art, performance studies) is well-articulated in the existing publications.
