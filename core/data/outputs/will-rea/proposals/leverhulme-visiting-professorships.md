## ⚠️ Key Mechanic: Rea is the HOST, Not the Fellow

This scheme works differently from all others in the matches list. Rea applies as the **UK institution host**, bringing a visiting professor FROM overseas to Leeds. The visiting professor is the fellow; Rea and the University of Leeds are the applicant. This is a mechanism for building international collaborative networks — directly addressing Rea's identified gap of "limited international collaborative network outside UK–Nigeria axis."

## 1. Project Framing

The Visiting Professorships scheme funds an overseas senior professor to spend 3–12 months in residence at a UK institution. For Rea, this is an opportunity to build a formal research partnership with a scholar whose expertise complements his own — and whose presence at Leeds would produce collaborative outputs (joint papers, a symposium, a jointly developed grant bid) that neither could produce independently. The strategic purpose is twofold: (1) establish a formal international research partnership that strengthens future AHRC, Leverhulme RPG, or ERC Synergy applications; (2) address the "limited international collaborative network" gap in Rea's profile.

The case for the visiting professor must explain why Leeds specifically (not Oxford, SOAS, or any other UK institution), why this particular scholar, and what the visit will produce that ongoing email exchange and conference encounters cannot.

## 2. Collaboration Profiles (Who to Invite)

Rea must identify the specific person to invite from his own network. This document describes the **profile** of an ideal visiting professor. Three profiles:

### Profile A: A Nigerian or West African art historian at an African university

An established art historian or cultural studies scholar based at a Nigerian university (e.g. University of Lagos, University of Ibadan, Obafemi Awolowo University, or a Ghanaian/Senegalese equivalent) with expertise in Yoruba visual culture, masquerade, or West African heritage. Leeds is the right host because of HMI's sculpture resources, the School of Fine Art's art history programme, and Rea's 30-year Ekiti corpus. Collaborative outputs: a jointly developed paper on comparative methodologies in African art history; a symposium connecting Leeds and Nigerian scholars; a jointly developed AHRC or Leverhulme RPG bid for a comparative project.

### Profile B: A performance studies or anthropology scholar from a Horizon Europe country

A scholar from a European institution (Netherlands, Germany, France, Belgium) working on masquerade, performance, or postcolonial cultural heritage with a West African regional focus. Collaborative outputs: a joint paper connecting performance studies and art history methodology; a seed partnership for a future HERA or ERC Synergy application; a symposium that builds a European network around African cultural heritage.

### Profile C: A museum studies or heritage scholar with African institutional experience

A curator or heritage studies academic (possibly US-based, Australian, or European) with direct experience of African collection display, decolonisation of African art in Western institutions, or heritage management in sub-Saharan Africa. Collaborative outputs: a joint symposium with the Henry Moore Institute; a jointly developed research framework for the John Randle Museum; a contribution to the Cambridge UP *Africa displaying / Displaying Africa* manuscript.

## 4. Methodology Notes

The application must articulate what the visitor will DO during the period at Leeds, not just who they are. The Leverhulme requires evidence that the visit will produce new research, not just an exchange of existing work. Typical activities to include: a public lecture series; a graduate seminar; a co-organised symposium; joint fieldwork preparation or analysis sessions; collaborative paper writing. The HMI's visiting research programme and the LAHRI seminar infrastructure at Leeds provide ready-made venues.

## 7. Budget Considerations

£10,000–£150,000 covers the visiting professor's salary (replacement or supplement), travel to and from the UK, accommodation in Leeds, and research expenses during the visit. A 6-month visit at professorial level might cost approximately £30,000–£50,000 (salary supplement, flights, Leeds accommodation, subsistence). A full academic year could reach £80,000–£100,000. Leeds would typically contribute to on-costs. Confirm with the Research Office.
