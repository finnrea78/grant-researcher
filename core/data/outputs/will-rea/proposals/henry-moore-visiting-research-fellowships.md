**This requires resolution before applying.** Three options:

1. **Contact HMI directly** — ask whether Leeds-based researchers are eligible. Given Rea's prior relationship with HMI, this is a reasonable enquiry. HMI may have a different process for locally-based researchers who want intensive access to the research programme.

3. **Apply as a researcher coming from a project elsewhere** — if Rea is spending time at a different institution (e.g. UEA Sainsbury Research Unit, or visiting a Nigerian partner), a Visiting Fellowship at HMI during that period may make more logistical sense.

**The rest of this document proceeds on the assumption that HMI confirms eligibility or that the scheme can be interpreted broadly.** If HMI advises that local Leeds researchers are ineligible, pivot immediately to the Research and Travel Grants alignment (a separate proposal document can be generated with `/propose henry-moore "Research and Travel Grants"`).

## 1. Project Framing

The Henry Moore Foundation exists to advance knowledge and understanding of sculpture in its broadest sense. Rea is one of a very small number of UK art historians who has spent three decades conducting sustained empirical and theoretical research into a non-Western sculptural tradition — the Epa and Egungun masquerade corpus of Ekiti, Yorubaland (Nigeria). His 2022 chapter in the Yale University Press *Bamigboye* volume, his 2019 *African Arts* structural analysis, and his forthcoming monograph at James Currey Publications represent the most detailed art-historical account of a living African sculptural tradition currently available in Anglophone scholarship.

HMI's research programme has long engaged with the question of what sculpture *is* — its materiality, its relation to the body, its social and political life. Rea's work addresses these questions through a single sustained field site over 30 years, producing the kind of longitudinal depth rarely available to sculpture studies. The framing for HMI is straightforward: Rea should be positioned not as an Africanist who is requesting access to a Western institution's resources, but as a **sculpture studies scholar** who happens to work on an African tradition — and whose theoretical contributions to questions of materiality, performance, and the social life of objects are directly relevant to HMI's intellectual agenda.

## 2. Research Question Suggestions

### Option A: The Sculptors of Ekiti — Manuscript Completion Residency

**Angle:** Use the fellowship month as a dedicated writing and research period to complete the "Sculptors of Ekiti" chapter (already peer-reviewed and submitted to Picton's *William Fagg and African Art* volume) and advance the "No Event, No History" monograph. Frame the residency as using HMI's specialist library and archive to enrich the manuscript's discussion of Ekiti sculptural form in comparative context.

**Why it fits the funder:** The chapter is explicitly about the conditions of sculptural creativity in a specific African tradition — directly within HMI's remit. The Fagg editorial context (William Fagg was a central figure in British engagement with African sculpture) creates a direct link between Rea's work and HMI's own institutional history. Access to HMI's library (which holds major resources on non-Western sculpture and on Fagg's collection activity) could produce specific manuscript improvements.

**What the project would do:** One month of intensive writing at HMI. Specific deliverable: a completed manuscript chapter and an identifiable advance on the James Currey monograph. Archival access to HMI's Fagg-related holdings and non-Western sculpture library would provide comparative material and bibliographic depth not available at Leeds.

### Option B: Sculpture, Materiality, and the Body in Yoruba Masquerade — A Theoretical Intervention

**Angle:** Frame the fellowship around Rea's theoretical strand — his published work on materiality (Bloomsbury 2017), sculpture as archaeology (Ashgate 2011), and anthropology of art — and use the month at HMI to develop a new theoretical essay on what Yoruba masquerade sculpture contributes to foundational questions in sculpture studies: the distinction between object and performance, the animated body, the role of concealment.

**Why it fits the funder:** HMI's research programme has actively engaged with exactly these questions — the body and sculpture, animism and form, the boundaries of the sculptural object. Rea's empirical base (30 years of masquerade fieldwork) combined with his methodological range (anthropology of art, postcolonial theory, structural analysis) would produce the kind of theoretical contribution that HMI's scholarly community would actively engage with. A seminar or public lecture during the residency would be a natural output.

**What the project would do:** One month at HMI producing: (1) a draft theoretical essay on masquerade and sculpture studies; (2) an HMI research seminar or public lecture; (3) an engagement with HMI's curators and fellows on the limits of Western sculpture categories. An essay in *The Sculpture Journal* would be the intended publication destination — Rea has published there before (2002, 2010).

### Option C: The John Randle Museum — Comparative Methods in Museum Display of Sculpture

**Angle:** Frame the residency around Rea's current live project — the John Randle Museum of Yoruba Cultural Heritage, Lagos (where he is project curator and academic lead). Use the month at HMI to conduct comparative research into how Western sculpture institutions display and contextualise African sculptural traditions — examining HMI's own collection, display choices, and catalogue methodology as a comparative case for the John Randle Museum's curatorial planning.

**Why it fits the funder:** This framing speaks directly to HMI's own institutional practice — not just its research programme. It positions Rea as a practitioner using HMI expertise to improve a major new African cultural institution. HMI has an established interest in display and collections (it holds works, loans from the British Museum, and has a significant publication programme). The comparative dimension gives the application a clear "why HMI specifically" answer.

**What the project would do:** One month of comparative curatorial research at HMI. Deliverable: a comparative display methodology report informing the John Randle Museum's design process; a public lecture on sculpted objects in institutional display, connecting Leeds and Lagos; and material for the Cambridge UP *Africa displaying / Displaying Africa* manuscript (which directly addresses this theme).

## 4. Methodology Notes

HMI values empirically grounded research that engages with sculpture as a material and social phenomenon. Rea's combination of long-term ethnographic fieldwork and formal art-historical analysis is well-suited to HMI's interdisciplinary outlook. The structural approach demonstrated in the 2019 *African Arts* paper — close formal analysis combined with ethnographic contextualisation — resonates with HMI's own approach to understanding objects.

The key methodological framing for the application is **comparative sculpture studies**: positioning Rea's African corpus as a contribution to the broader field of sculpture studies, not a specialist African art studies project. This requires explicit comparative reach in the application — connecting Yoruba masquerade sculpture to debates in Western sculpture history (materiality, animated form, the performative object) that HMI scholars will recognise.

One gap to address: Rea's published work rarely engages explicitly with sculpture studies literature (Krauss, Potts, Wood, Marioni, etc.). The application should show awareness of this literature and position Rea's empirical work as offering a corrective or expansion to its Western assumptions. This is not a deep methodological weakness — it is a framing issue easily addressed in the application narrative.

The John Randle Museum project is a powerful concrete impact statement: Rea is directly applying sculpture studies knowledge to the design of a major new African public museum. This is knowledge exchange at scale — not a paper, but an institution. HMI would benefit reputationally from association with this project, and Rea's residency could produce a seminar or public event connecting Leeds and Lagos that HMI could publicise.

## 7. Budget Considerations

The scheme covers accommodation, travel, and subsistence for the month. Because Rea is Leeds-based, these costs may be negligible or zero. There is no indication that the scheme offers a stipend or salary supplement — it is a resource-access fellowship, not a salary-replacement mechanism.

This means the fellowship's value to Rea is **not financial** but **structural**: it provides a formal institutional context for a month of dedicated research, with access to HMI's specialist library, curatorial expertise, archive, and intellectual community. The application should make this structural value explicit — show that HMI's library and staff expertise cannot be replicated through a library loan or a day visit.

Do not request costs that Rea will not incur. If there is a budget section, propose only genuinely needed research costs — e.g. reproduction rights for images used in the manuscript, specialist materials not held in HMI's library, or costs for a public lecture (printing, hosting).

Do not propose fieldwork costs within this fellowship — those belong in a separate Research and Travel Grants application.
