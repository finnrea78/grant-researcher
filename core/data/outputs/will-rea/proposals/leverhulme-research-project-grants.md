## 1. Project Framing

The Leverhulme Research Project Grant funds team-based research — a PI at a UK HEI, up to 3 co-applicants, with at least 75% of the budget spent on research staff (research assistant, PhD students, or overseas researcher). This is a different model from the fellowships: it requires assembling a project team, identifying a clear research question that benefits from collaborative effort, and delivering outputs over up to 5 years. At up to £500,000, it is the most financially significant individual Leverhulme route available to Rea and would directly address the "no major PI grant in 15 years" gap.

The outline application stage is currently paused (resuming autumn 2026), so this is not an immediate action item — but it requires significant preparation time and Rea should begin developing the project concept now.

## 2. Research Question Suggestions

### Option A: The Sculptors of Ekiti — A Comparative Study of Creativity, Patronage, and Tradition (3–5 years)

A 3–5 year comparative project examining the social, material, and historical conditions of sculptural production in Ekiti across the twentieth and twenty-first centuries. PI: Rea. Research assistant: a postdoctoral scholar in Yoruba art or West African cultural history. Project outputs: a co-authored monograph (building on Rea's existing Bamigboye and Epa masquerade corpus), a fieldwork archive, and a public-facing digital resource. The project leverages Rea's 30-year longitudinal access to Ikole Ekiti — an evidential base no competing team possesses.

### Option B: African Display — Yoruba Cultural Heritage in International Contexts (3 years)

A 3-year project examining how Yoruba art objects are displayed, interpreted, and contested across multiple institutional contexts: the John Randle Museum (Lagos), the Horniman Museum (London), the British Museum, and one or two European institutions. PI: Rea. Co-applicant: a museum studies or curatorial scholar from a UK institution. Research assistant: postdoctoral scholar or research administrator. Outputs: a co-authored monograph (*Africa displaying / Displaying Africa* expanded beyond the CUP Elements format), exhibition consultancy, and policy recommendations for decolonising African art display. This project connects Rea's live museum project (John Randle) to a comparative academic study with strong public impact.

### Option C: Masquerade as Political Institution — A Comparative African Study (5 years)

A larger comparative project extending Rea's Ekiti masquerade research to other West African masquerade traditions (Gelede, Egungun in diaspora, Ijo water masquerade) as parallel political institutions. Requires a co-investigator with expertise in a second tradition and a research assistant. More ambitious in scope but less immediately grounded in Rea's existing corpus. Strongest for AHRC rather than Leverhulme given the breadth.

## 7. Budget Considerations

At £500,000 maximum over 5 years, a realistic budget might include: postdoctoral research assistant (3 years, ~£40,000/year = £120,000); fieldwork costs for PI and researcher (~£30,000 total); conference and dissemination (~£15,000); teaching replacement for PI (up to 33% over 3 years, ~£60,000); associated research costs (travel, reproduction, archive fees, ~£25,000). Total: ~£250,000 for a 3-year project. A 5-year project with a longer postdoc could reach £400,000. Leverhulme does NOT fund open-access publication charges or overhead.
