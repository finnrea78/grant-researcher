## 2. Research Question Suggestions

### Option A: Ekiti fieldwork for the masquerade monograph

A targeted fieldwork trip to Ikole Ekiti (2–3 weeks) to conduct follow-up interviews with sculptors, carvers, and masquerade custodians in direct support of the peer-review revision of "No Event, No History." Specific fieldwork gaps to address: updates to the longitudinal record since the last fieldwork trip; photographic documentation of objects described in manuscript chapters; verification of oral history accounts. Deliverable: a revised monograph chapter or sections incorporating new field material. The BA 2006 grant "Ibadan 1960" addressed a comparable fieldwork requirement — this application is a direct continuation of the same research programme.

### Option B: UK archival research — British Museum and Horniman collections

A focussed visit to the British Museum Department of Africa, Oceania and the Americas and the Horniman Museum to examine unpublished archival material relating to Ekiti sculptors and Epa masquerade objects — accession records, colonial correspondence, and field photographs. Links directly to the submitted chapter in Picton (ed.), *William Fagg and African Art*, which draws on Fagg's fieldwork records. Deliverable: archival materials feeding into the Picton volume chapter and the masquerade monograph. This is the lowest-cost option — well within £10,000 for UK travel and accommodation.

### Option C: Lagos and Ibadan — Cambridge UP manuscript fieldwork

A research trip to Lagos and Ibadan to gather documentation for *Africa displaying / Displaying Africa* (Cambridge UP). Archives at Lagos State heritage collections; fieldwork at the John Randle Museum of Yoruba Cultural Heritage (Rea is project curator); visits to institutional collections in Ibadan and Ife. Deliverable: archival and photographic documentation feeding into the CUP manuscript and the museum's interpretive framework. Strong impact narrative: the research informs a live national museum project. Return flights to Nigeria (c. £700–900) would constitute the main cost — ensure the total stays within £10,000 including accommodation and ground transport.

**Recommendation:** Option A or C, depending on which manuscript is at a more critical juncture at the time of application.

## 4. Methodology Notes

The scheme is designed for exactly this use case: targeted travel and fieldwork costs for humanities researchers. The application should name specific archives or field sites to be visited, specific research questions that cannot be answered from Leeds, and the specific manuscript sections that will benefit. Reviewers reward proportionality — a defined trip with a defined output, not an open-ended expedition.
