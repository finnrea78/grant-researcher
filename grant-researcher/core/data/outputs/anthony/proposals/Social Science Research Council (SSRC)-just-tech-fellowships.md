## 2. Research Question Suggestions

### Option A: Algorithmic Fairness in Financial Signal Processing Systems

How do the design choices embedded in financial signal processing algorithms — filtering, noise reduction, feature selection — systematically advantage or disadvantage different populations in automated financial decision-making? This angle leverages Prof. Constantinides' deep technical knowledge of DSP and financial engineering to interrogate, from the inside, the technical architectures that social scientists critique from the outside. The project would produce a technically grounded taxonomy of how filter design choices propagate bias in financial systems.

### Option B: The Signal and the Noise — Who Gets Filtered Out?

What happens when signal processing techniques developed for communications engineering are applied to human behavioural data in fintech and insurtech contexts? This project would examine how the conceptual framework of "signal" vs. "noise" — foundational to Prof. Constantinides' career — becomes a tool of social sorting when applied to people rather than electromagnetic waves. It would combine technical analysis with critical reflection on the social implications of treating human variability as noise to be removed.

### Option C: Democratic Governance of Technical Standards in Signal Processing

How are international technical standards in signal processing and communications (IEEE, IET) governed, and whose interests do they serve? Drawing on Prof. Constantinides' extensive experience in standards bodies, professional societies (first President of EURASIP, IEEE Board of Governors), this project would examine the political economy of technical standard-setting as a site of power that shapes technological futures. This is a science-and-technology-studies (STS) angle that leverages institutional knowledge few researchers possess.
