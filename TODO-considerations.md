# Product Considerations — Grant Scout

Non-technical feedback and key points raised by academics and contacts.

---

## Eligibility Filtering

- Do not show grants the researcher is ineligible for.
- Do not show grants they have already applied for.
- Filter by career stage — some grants are too junior or too senior for the researcher's level.
- This is a core trust-builder. Irrelevant results damage credibility.

---

## Smaller and Less-Visible Grants

- UKRI and major funders are the obvious sources — but not the only ones.
- Examples of overlooked grants:
  - **European Space Agency** — has grant programmes that go unseen
  - **Call the Midwife (UK TV show)** — provides grants specifically to medics
  - Sector-specific grants that do not appear in mainstream databases
- Goal: surface these hidden opportunities alongside mainstream ones.
- **Own Grant DB** — maintain an internal database of curated grants, updated regularly.

---

## FEC (Full Economic Costing) — Money Split

FEC is a UK university funding model. Grant amounts are not always what they seem.

- Example: A grant listed at **£800 FEC (80/20)** means:
  - Total grant value = **£800**
  - Funder pays **80%** = £640
  - University co-funds **20%** = £160
- This is **not** a £1,000 grant — the university contribution is not additional money.
- The app must display the correct split clearly so researchers understand the real funder value.

---

## Success Likelihood and Ranking

- Researchers need to prioritise which grants to pursue.
- Key request: **show likelihood of success** for each grant.
- Allow researchers to **rank grant proposals** against each other.
- This eases the decision-making process — a major pain point.

---

## Learning from Grant Outcomes

- Study **successful grants** from UKRI and other funders to identify patterns.
- Collect data on **unsuccessful grants** directly from researchers.
- Look for correlation between grant characteristics and outcomes.
- This could power a smarter ranking and recommendation system over time.
